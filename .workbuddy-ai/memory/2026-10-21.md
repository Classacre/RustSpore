# 2026-10-21

## Cross-package resolution, and a measured negative

`PackageIndex` in `dbpf` reads only the header and the index block, then seeks for a
payload. `Spore_Graphics` is 995 MB on disk with a 1.2 MB index, so indexing all 82 packages
takes about 2 seconds and roughly 80 MB of index data, against 5.75 GB for opening them.

`PackageSet` in `assets.rs` holds those indexes behind a `Mutex` (a Bevy resource must be
`Sync`, so `RefCell` is not an option) and resolves a resource by type and instance. Set
`RUSTSPORE_PACKAGES` to a `;`-separated list of packages or directories to attach one.

### A type of 0 means unspecified

Keys with type `0x00000000` still carry real instances: `0x00000000BE37F695` is an RW4 in
`Spore_Graphics`. Matching on the instance alone resolves it. In `Spore_Graphics` this
raised resolved model props from 2,072 of 2,613 to 2,443, and LOD slots from 2,589 to 3,400.

The remaining 170 are **dangling**: the instances exist in no package in the install. I
added `--locate <instance> [type]` so that question gets an answer rather than a guess.

### The texture gap did not close, and that is the result

I expected the 1,009 textureless model props in `Spore_Content` to inherit a texture from
their parent once parents could be reached across packages. They do not:

- 0 of 1,009 gain a texture from any ancestor, up to 4 hops, with all 82 packages indexed.
- Every one of the 1,162 props in `Spore_Content` has a copy in `PatchData` (the patch
  overlay covers all of them), and the copy carries no texture where the original has none.
- So those parts are not textured by the prop graph at all. They must be painted by the
  creature layer, which assigns a skin per creature rather than per part. That is Phase 3.

The count stays at 153 of 1,162, and the docs now say why instead of promising that
cross-package resolution would fix it.

### Also

`index::detect_with_len` exists because `detect` scored contiguity against `bytes.len()`,
which is the slice length. For a buffer holding only the index block, every candidate
scored out of range and valid layouts were rejected. The full reader passes its own length
and is unchanged.

## The creature rig reads

`creature_data::parse` now decodes the `data0` rig table into `CreaturePart`: one 0x8C-byte
row per part, holding a part prop instance plus a rotation and translation.

Measured, before writing any of it:

- The 3x3 at offset 48 is **orthonormal on 31,704 of 31,704 rows** across the 1,022
  creature files in `Spore_Content` (row norms 1.0, pairwise dots 0.0, tolerance 0.01).
- The three floats at 84 are a translation: median largest component 0.90, max 17.25.
- The words at 132 and 136 are the part prop instance, **high half first**. Every one of
  the 31,704 rows resolves to a prop in the install, all in `PatchData`.
- 27,959 of 31,704 part models exist (**88.2%**); 773 of 1,022 creatures have every part.

`mesh::append_transformed` rotates then translates, rotates normals without translating
them, and offsets indices so one draw call covers the whole creature.

### Transforms are absolute, not hierarchical

I expected a parent chain and it is not one. For `0x406B62000660EACA` the assembled bounds
are x 0.156..0.627, y -0.643..-0.039, z 0.173..1.117 (radius 0.472); the merged model the
game ships for the same instance is x 0.119..0.615, y -0.654..-0.008, z 0.126..0.934
(radius 0.404). Same volume, no accumulation needed.

### The silhouette claim was wrong, and pixels were the reason

I first compared the two by rendering each and taking an intersection over union of
non-background pixels: 0.178, which I wrote up as "the rig puts parts in the right volume
but something else differs, probably a per-part scale". That was wrong.

The viewer normalises each mesh by its own radius and centres it on its own bounds, so two
identical shapes render at different sizes and positions. The pixel metric measured the
framing, not the geometry.

The metric that needs no camera: nearest-neighbour distance between vertices, as a share of
the merged model's diagonal. Over the **93 creatures in `Spore_Content` with both a
complete rig and a merged model**: min 0.9%, p10 1.3%, **median 2.1%**, p90 5.6%, max
10.4%. The rig reproduces the shape.

Lesson: a comparison that depends on a renderer's framing is not a measurement of geometry.
When two things can be compared directly, compare them directly.

### One bug worth remembering

`resolve_prop` still called `find_by_instance` directly for the prop itself, so every
creature part failed with "no prop with instance ..." even though `--locate` found it.
`--locate` existed by then, which is exactly why the failure took two minutes instead of
an hour: it separated "the resolver is wrong" from "the reference is dangling".

## New modes this session

`--creature-parts` prints, per rig row, the row's bounding box against the part model's own
extent, the ratio, the five unestablished floats and where the row lands in world space.
`--creature-overlap` compares the assembly against the merged model by vertex distance.
`--creature-info` prints both sets of bounds side by side.

## A creature's skin is keyed by its instance low half

No prop names a creature's texture, which is why 1,009 model props in `Spore_Content` look
textureless. The skin is a RASTER whose instance shares the creature's **low** half and
differs in the high half: creature `0x40666200D1B4BB56` has `0x40662900D1B4BB56` (512x512
DXT5, the atlas with eyes and mouths), `0x40662800/01` (256x256 colour), `0x40662C00/01`
(256x256 DXT1 detail) and `0x40662901` (256x256 raw).

**366 of 1,022** creatures ship one; the other 656 have none and need the paint system.

Among the 366 the largest raster is always the atlas, so `creature_skin` picks by area
rather than by a guessed ident. The viewer binds it for `creature=<instance>`.

A part's own RW4 container holds no Raster (checked `0x4060600006B00D54`), so the skin atlas
is the only texture a creature has and every part's UVs point into it. One material covers
the whole assembly.

Rendered a 60-part creature at 24,113 vertices with its skin, and a 21-part one with a
512x512 atlas: eyes and mouths land on the right parts, which is the first visual proof
that the UVs and the rig agree.

## The paint palette, and a decompressor that was never right

The decompressed header of a `.creaturedata` is 0x44 bytes. Nine floats at offset 32 are
three RGB triples: the creature's paint colours.

Established by checking, not by eye: for five creatures that ship a skin, every palette
colour appears in that skin's pixel set to within one step of 8/255. So the skin was painted
with them.

For the 656 creatures with no shipped skin, the palette is all there is. The viewer now
paints them with the first triple instead of borrowing a package texture, and stops falling
back to that texture for creatures specifically. `0x406262008933CC09` renders cyan,
`0x406262004BF23F8D` pale yellow.

### The bug underneath

Reading the palette returned -8e18. The RLE decompressor emitted `current[0]` for every
literal and never advanced its index, so the "decompressed" buffer was one byte repeated.
The parse still consumed exactly, because that buffer was kept only "for information" and
nothing read it. Eight months of format docs said 100% decoded while one field was garbage.

Lesson: a value kept "for information" is untested. Either read it somewhere or delete it.
A unit test now covers a chunk with a repeat escape.

150 tests pass (dbpf 20, spore-core 130).
