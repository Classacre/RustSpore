## What the listing establishes

The producer's input depth is a 32-bit float argument (`param_4`). After its four register saves it is addressed at `[ESP+24]`, with offsets shifting when arguments are pushed. At 0xE6A4F0 the initial FLD loads this argument. The d==6 call stores and pops it at 0xE6A532, while the other path discards it at 0xE6A546. Thus neither leaves this initial depth on the x87 stack for the later body. Separately, the early d==7 branch loads it at 0xE6A477, passes it by FSTP at 0xE6A4AC or 0xE6A4D5, and returns. Other early-return submissions similarly load and FSTP an argument copy, e.g. 0xE6A5C6/0xE6A5DD and 0xE6A680/0xE6A695. The intervening 0xE6A548..590 x87 sequence computes and pops a separate distance argument for E673A0; it is not the retained candidate depth.

The later branch body loads the depth from its stack argument at sites including 0xE6A82E, 859, 902, 969, 9B5, 9FF, AAC8, AB22, ACE9, ACF2, AD14, AD2A, AD73 and ADAA (addresses after the first retain the E6 prefix). It passes depth to most later E59B40 calls using `FST float ptr [ESP]`, which copies ST0 to a binary32 argument without popping it. At the common tail 0xE6ADBF, `FSTP float ptr [ESP]` supplies E6A2B0 and pops ST0. Branch-specific FLDs make the paths converge with a live value; the relevant paths should be checked against the listing, not assumed from Ghidra's synthetic `float10` expressions.

E59B40 q493 has no x87 instructions and no calls; it reads the submitted binary32 using MOVSS, compares with COMISS, and writes with MOVSS. Thus, for normal non-faulting execution, that call does not itself consume, produce, or alter ST0. A decompiler expression treating its result as `float10` is not an actual floating return: the live value survives the `FST` and call. In particular, the stores at 0xE6A845, 0xE6A8A7, 0xE6A8D1, 0xE6A91B, 0xE6A97E, 0xE6A9CC, 0xE6AA31, 0xE6AAE5, 0xE6AB0F, 0xE6AB63, 0xE6ABC2, 0xE6AC3F, 0xE6AC7A, 0xE6ACD6, 0xE6AD55 and 0xE6AD9A retain ST0 across E59B40.

E571C0 is called with retained ST0 at 0xE6A874, 0xE6AB9A and 0xE6ABF5. Its own listing has no x87 instructions. When B+358 is -1 it calls E4ECC0 at 0xE571E2; q666 shows that callee has no x87 instructions or calls. Thus E571C0 preserves ST0 on both paths under normal execution. At 0xE6A881 a path explicitly pops ST0 before E527C0, then reloads the argument at 0xE6A888; similarly 0xE6AD0D pops before E503A0 and 0xE6AD14 reloads. E573E0 at 0xE6AA99 runs before the relevant depth load, and the subsequent paths reload at 0xE6AAC8 or 0xE6AD2A.

## Modeling limits

Under a normal x87 environment, a valid live stack, no argument alias mutation, and normal completion of E571C0 and E4ECC0, later submissions and tail receive stores derived from the loaded input depth; E59B40 does not replace that value with a return. This is a much stronger boundary than the earlier decompiler-only concern, but it is not a proof of bit-identical behavior for every float. `FLD` converts binary32 to x87 extended and `FST`/`FSTP` convert to binary32 under the x87 control environment; signaling NaNs, exception masks, rounding/control state, stack faults, and the SSE comparison environment require explicit admission or observation. Stores do not replace ST0 with their rounded binary32 result. Repeated reloads can differ if the stack argument is mutated through aliasing or an external call; the current argument should be observed at each native load unless non-aliasing is established. E59B40 can mutate the selection and its pointed-to output could theoretically alias other inputs. This trace alone does not prove all external calls preserve memory, nonlocal state, or the x87 stack.
