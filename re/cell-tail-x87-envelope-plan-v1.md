# Controlled x87 envelope: architectural design only

The [assembly review skeleton](cell-tail-x87-envelope-draft-v1.asm) proposes one uninterrupted x86 test envelope with three distinct 16-byte-aligned, writable 512-byte FXSAVE-format buffers. It saves the caller's legacy x87/MMX/XMM/MXCSR state without executing a waiting x87 instruction, rejects any pre-existing x87 exception flags, stack fault, exception-summary or busy status, then initializes an isolated finite fixture with x87 control `027F` and MXCSR `1F80`. To keep preservation claims narrow, the eventual host should also require the saved abridged tag byte to be zero and saved TOP to be zero before changing the environment. It captures the fixture before and after a reviewed source thunk, restores the saved image with FXRSTOR, and only then reports validation. The full linked envelope and thunk must be reviewed before assembly or execution.

Intel's [FXSAVE instruction reference](https://cdrdv2-public.intel.com/812383/253666-sdm-vol-2a.pdf) states that FXSAVE does not wait for pending unmasked x87 exceptions and retains the processor state; its one-bit-per-register tag reports empty versus nonempty, not the original full tag class. The Intel [combined manuals](https://cdrdv2-public.intel.com/868137/325462-089-sdm-vol-1-2abcd-3abcd-4.pdf) describe a binary32 subnormal load setting the denormal flag under a masked exception while still loading the value. Thus checking merely TOP is insufficient, and an observed denormal sticky flag is not by itself a mismatch. The design verifies both TOP and abridged FTW on entry and exit, while separately retaining status. `027F` masks the x87 exceptions; MXCSR `1F80` masks SSE exceptions and clears DAZ/FTZ. No inference is made about retail controls.

For a candidate finite binary32 depth, independently compare the E59-substitute stack word to the original bits, including positive and negative subnormals and zero. After source return require FCW `027F`, FTW zero, TOP zero, no x87 stack fault or exception-summary, unchanged MXCSR control, correct ESP and nonvolatile registers, and bounded output/log. Other x87 sticky flags must be recorded rather than presented as propagated through the Rust model. Restore the saved architectural state before any C/library call that could use floating-point state. The checked cache hosts documented in [the retained host note](cell-capability-retained-host-evidence-v1.md) initialize an explicit CW and inspect TOP and MXCSR, but their reported checks do not establish this stronger complete tag/status and caller-state protocol.

This is not yet a safe executable host. FXSAVE/FXRSTOR preserve legacy architectural state, not AVX upper lanes or every OS extended-state component. The eventual isolated process must establish CPU FXSR/SSE2 and OS support, alignment and lifetimes, and prohibit any compiler-generated FP/SIMD or calls between the capture and restore except the reviewed source/stubs. An exception or bad return before FXRSTOR has no demonstrated in-process recovery; a failed isolated child must be terminated, and the parent must not claim restoration. Debugger attachment, fault-handler behavior, stable files and nonhermetic dependencies remain open admissions. A full-tag reconstruction, arbitrary nonempty initial stack, pending unmasked exception and asynchronous recovery are expressly unsupported. No original instructions have run.

## Dedicated wrapper draft

[The MASM wrapper](cell-tail-x87-wrapper-review-v1.asm) is review-only and has been assembled solely for linked inspection. It provides separate `saved`, `before`, `after` and `restored` 512-byte images, tests nonnull/aligned pointers, rejects saved FSW mask `B8FFh` and nonempty abridged tag, creates the fixture, invokes a no-argument cdecl thunk, captures immediately, restores, then captures a restoration image. Buffer disjointness, accessibility, CPU/OS support, thunk's balanced ESP and preserved EBP, and absence of faults are caller admissions rather than checks. The thunk can clobber caller-saved GPRs, x87 and XMM state; FXRSTOR is intended to restore legacy FP/SIMD state, while AVX upper lanes and other extended state are excluded. A fault before FXRSTOR cannot be recovered by this wrapper.

Before calling it, the eventual host must verify all four complete address ranges are writable, 16-byte aligned, nonoverlapping and do not wrap; validate its thunk and buffers; and perform the debugger/exception preflight. Independent review must confirm the MASM-generated EBP frame and indirect call, preserved nonvolatile registers and stack in the linked binary. A separate controlled stack/register sentinel must detect thunk ABI violations; this draft assumes them, it does not detect or repair them. The capture areas must remain immutable except for their prescribed writes.

Validation after normal return must be field-wise: `before` FCW `027F`, FSW zero, abridged FTW zero and MXCSR `1F80`; `after` FCW unchanged, TOP zero, FTW zero, stack-fault/exception-summary clear, and MXCSR control bits unchanged. Record the six x87 exception flags and SSE sticky flags separately. For this exact finite FLD/FSTP route, DE may occur on subnormal input; any other unexpected x87 flag must fail or be investigated rather than silently accepted. Compare `saved` and `restored` architectural FCW, FSW, FTW, MXCSR and defined XMM registers; review additional instruction/data-pointer and opcode fields against the actual CPU/FXRSTOR contract before making restoration claims. Do not compare reserved bytes, software-available bytes or payload slots for empty x87 registers as if they were defined state. No byte-for-byte FXSAVE image equality is claimed.

Independent review identified that the first wrapper draft overwrote the thunk's EAX with wrapper status. The current draft adds a distinct caller-owned four-byte `thunk_result` slot and writes EAX immediately after the thunk returns. Wrapper EAX zero means the capture and restore sequence completed; the host must separately check `thunk_result`, field-wise validation and the logged source/stub outcomes. On pre-call failure the result slot remains unchanged. Its validity, lifetime and non-aliasing with all FP images, code, source buffers and logs are explicit caller admissions and must be checked by the eventual host. Independent review confirmed the conservative FSW mask and fixture-error restore logic at source level, but linked assembly, call slot/ABI, full memory disjointness and field-wise restoration still require verification.

Caller-owned memory ranges, mapping, disjointness and fault behavior are admissions; no page protection or safe fault recovery has been established.
